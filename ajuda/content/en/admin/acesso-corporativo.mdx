# Corporate access: setting up

By default, people sign in to ITS with email and password. With **corporate access** (SSO, or "single sign-on"), they can also sign in with their company Microsoft or Google account, without creating another password. The [provider](/en/referencia/glossario/#provedor) confirms who the person is, and ITS only receives the confirmation.

The screen is in **Administration > Corporate access** and only shows for people with the **Configure corporate access** permission (out of the box, the Administrator). This page covers turning the feature on; [day-to-day use](/en/admin/acesso-corporativo-no-dia-a-dia/) has its own page.

<Atencao>
  Everything comes turned off. Turning a provider on changes nothing for anyone <Regra id="RN-97">until you choose a sign-in mode with SSO</Regra>.
</Atencao>

## What do I need before I start?

Your organization's IT registers the application with Microsoft (Entra), Google or both and gives you the **Client (application) ID**, the **Client secret** and, for Microsoft, the ID of the [tenant](/en/referencia/glossario/#tenant). IT's steps are in [Corporate sign-in](/en/ti/login-corporativo/).

Also check the **System address** in **Settings > Notifications and email**: it must be filled in and start with `https://`.

## Which emails does ITS accept?

In **Domains and outside email**, enter the organization's domains, one per line (for example `company.com`). With the list filled in, <Regra id="RN-95">only emails from those domains can be registered, or changed to</Regra>. With the list empty there's no restriction, and nothing changes for people who already use ITS. Corporate sign-in needs at least one domain.

- **Accept email from outside the domains** is for consultants and contractors. People with an outside email sign in with a password only, even with corporate sign-in turned on.
- People who already had an outside email keep signing in, and the screen lists them.

## How do I register a provider?

For each provider, fill in the client, the secret and, for Microsoft, the tenant, then click **Save provider**. The secret <Regra id="RN-97">is stored encrypted on the server and never shown back</Regra>. The screen also shows the [return address](/en/referencia/glossario/#endereco-de-retorno), which IT registers with the provider.

Then:

1. **Test address** checks only the network and the provider's address.
2. **Test by signing in** opens the provider's sign-in with your account. If that account's email is yours, it gets linked to your user. The test doesn't change your session.
3. Once the test passes, click **Turn on**.

Changing the client, the secret or the tenant turns the provider off and asks for a new test. The **Client (application) ID** and the **Tenant (directory) ID** <Regra id="RN-135">open hidden</Regra>: click the eye to see them.

## Can ITS use the photo from the corporate account?

Yes, if you turn on **Offer the profile photo** for the provider (it comes turned off). <Regra id="RN-144">ITS fetches the photo at sign-in and keeps it separately</Regra>, and it only shows if the person chooses it in **My account**. Turning it off deletes the photos kept from that provider. On Microsoft, the application needs one more permission, which IT adds: see [Corporate sign-in](/en/ti/login-corporativo/).

## Which sign-in mode should I choose?

| Mode | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Password only** (default) | Everyone signs in with email and password |
| **Password and SSO** | People who prefer can sign in with the corporate account; the password keeps working |
| **SSO only** | People with an organization email sign in only through the corporate account |

In **SSO only**, administrators keep the password as emergency access, in case the provider goes down or is misconfigured. People with an email outside the domains also keep their password. To choose SSO only, your own account must be linked: run the test with your email.

<Dica>
  A good order: start with **Password and SSO**, ask a few people to sign in with the button, and only then move to **SSO only**.
</Dica>

## Who gets an account at their first sign-in?

With automatic creation off (the default), only people you registered under **Users** can sign in. Anyone who isn't registered sees a message asking them to contact the administrator.

In large organizations, you can turn on **Create the account on first sign-in**. Then anyone who passes all the corporate sign-in rules <Regra id="RN-98">gets an account at their first sign-in</Regra>, without a password, with the **Starting role** and **Starting entity** you choose.

<Atencao>
  The starting role can never be administrative: the screen only lists roles without management permissions, and the server checks again when it creates the account.
</Atencao>

The provider is what limits who reaches ITS: IT restricts the application to a group on Microsoft, or to an organizational unit on Google (see [Corporate sign-in](/en/ti/login-corporativo/)). Without that, anyone in the domain gets an account.
