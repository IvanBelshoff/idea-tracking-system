# Merge similar ideas

When several people ask for the same improvement, ITS merges the requests into a single idea. Whoever asked later isn't dropped, and the queue has no duplicates. This is called convergence.

## Which idea becomes the main one?

The <Regra id="RN-84">oldest, by submission date</Regra>. It drives the workflow, and the others become **merged**: linked to the main idea, with no workflow of their own. They stop showing up separately in the queue and in the indicators, so nothing is counted twice.

You can only merge ideas with no work underway yet: Submitted or In review. An In progress idea already has steps, and that work would be lost.

## How do two ideas become one?

- The employee supports a similar idea instead of submitting another one.
- During triage, you mark two ideas as the same. In that case, a <Regra id="RN-85">justification is required</Regra> and stays in the history.

ITS only suggests similar ideas; it never merges them on its own.

## What happens to the people who asked?

Whoever had their idea merged <Regra id="RN-86">becomes a co-author of the main idea</Regra>, and their original text is kept for the team to read. The co-author <Regra id="RN-87">follows the main idea</Regra> in My ideas and gets every notification. The author of the main idea sees how many people asked for the same thing, which is a good signal for prioritizing.

For points and the Hall of fame, every co-author counts. By default, <Regra id="RN-89">the original author gets half</Regra>, and the rest is shared.

Merged ideas follow the main one: if it ends up not approved or is reopened, they go with it, and each co-author is notified with the same justification.

## Merged by mistake?

A merge <Regra id="RN-88">can be undone</Regra>: the idea gets its own workflow back, with its original submission date.
